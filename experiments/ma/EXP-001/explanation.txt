EXPERIMENT EXP-001 (MA): Moving Average Behavior Analysis
================================================================================

WHAT ARE WE TESTING?
--------------------
We're testing if Moving Averages can predict trend direction. A Moving Average
is a smoothed line that follows price:

- When price crosses ABOVE the MA → we expect price to continue UP
- When price crosses BELOW the MA → we expect price to continue DOWN

We tested 3 types of Moving Averages:
1. SMA (Simple) - averages all prices equally
2. EMA (Exponential) - gives more weight to recent prices (reacts faster)
3. WMA (Weighted) - linear weight to recent prices (between SMA and EMA)

We tested 6 different periods for each:
- 7, 9 (very short - 1.75 to 2.25 hours)
- 20 (short - 5 hours)
- 50 (medium - 12.5 hours)
- 100, 200 (long - 25 to 50 hours)

Total: 3 types × 6 periods = 18 combinations tested


WHY ARE WE TESTING THIS?
------------------------
In EXP-001 (RSI), we found that failures happen when trading AGAINST strong trend.
We want to use MA as a trend filter, but first we need to test:

1. Does MA actually predict trend direction?
2. Which MA type works best (SMA, EMA, or WMA)?
3. Which period works best (7, 9, 20, 50, 100, 200)?

This is ATOMIC testing - we test MA standalone before combining with RSI.


HOW DOES IT WORK?
-----------------
1. Signal Detection:
   - Price crosses ABOVE MA → "Uptrend" signal
   - Price crosses BELOW MA → "Downtrend" signal

2. What We Measured:
   - "Continuation Rate" = After cross, did price keep moving in that direction?
   - "Average Move" = How much did price move in expected direction?

3. Time Horizons: 3, 5, 10, 15, 20 bars

4. Data Periods:
   - In-Sample (training): 2020-2023 (140,105 candles)
   - Out-of-Sample (testing): 2024-2025 (69,942 candles)

WHAT DID WE LEARN?
------------------

1. ALL MA TYPES WORK SIMILARLY
   - SMA, EMA, WMA all show ~98-99% continuation rate
   - No clear winner - they all perform about the same
   - MA type doesn't matter much for trend identification

2. ALL PERIODS WORK SIMILARLY
   - Short (7, 9) and long (100, 200) all show ~98-99% continuation
   - Shorter periods = more signals but similar accuracy
   - Longer periods = fewer signals but similar accuracy

3. CONSISTENT ACROSS TIME PERIODS
   - In-sample: ~99.2-99.5% continuation rate
   - Out-of-sample: ~98.3-98.9% continuation rate
   - Small drop but still very high - pattern is real

4. MOVES ARE ABOVE MINIMUM PROFITABLE
   - In-sample: 67-76 bps average move
   - Out-of-sample: 52-59 bps average move
   - All well above 12 bps minimum

5. SIGNAL FREQUENCY VARIES BY PERIOD
   - Short periods (7, 9): 8,000-20,000 signals
   - Long periods (100, 200): 1,300-5,000 signals
   - Trade-off: More signals vs fewer but "stronger" trends


KEY INSIGHT: MA DOESN'T ADD MUCH VALUE FOR TREND
------------------------------------------------
The finding that ALL combinations show ~98-99% continuation rate suggests:

- Price tends to continue after ANY MA cross
- This might be because BTC trends strongly in general
- MA type and period don't differentiate much

This is similar to RSI showing 98-99% bounce rate - the market moves
in the expected direction almost always, the challenge is CAPTURING it.


IMPLICATIONS FOR RSI FILTER
---------------------------
Since all MA types/periods work similarly for trend identification,
we can pick any for the RSI filter. Suggested approach:

1. Use SMA (simplest, most common)
2. Use medium period like 50 (not too noisy, not too slow)
3. Or use shorter period like 20 for faster reaction

The choice doesn't matter much - what matters is HAVING a trend filter,
not WHICH specific MA we use.
